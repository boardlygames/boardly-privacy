Privacy Policy for Boardly: Game Night Classics

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Boardly: Game Night Classics ("the app") is an offline collection of classic board games such as chess, backgammon, Ludo, checkers, Snakes & Ladders, Reversi, tic-tac-toe and Four in a Row published by boardlygames ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the app does and does not do with information. The short version: we do not collect your name or any account, the app never reads the advertising ID, and it talks to only four outside services: Google AdMob, which shows ads, Google's Firebase, which tells us which games are played and when the app crashes, GitHub Pages, where we publish a small settings file, and Google Play, only if you choose to rate the app.

What we collect

Only usage statistics and crash reports, described below. They are not linked to your name, email address or any account. The app has no accounts, no sign-in and no servers of ours. We do not ask for your name, email address, contacts, photos, microphone or camera, and the app does not request those permissions or your device's location.

Usage statistics and crash reports

The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, two Google services, so we can see which games are played, keep the app working and fix crashes. They collect:

  • which games are played and how rounds go: the game, the number of players and computer players, the computer level, whether the standard rules were changed, whether the screen was upright or sideways, how a round ended (won, lost or a draw), when a saved game is resumed, how far a round had got when it was left unfinished (its minutes, and its moves or the pawns home or square reached), and when the rules or the rating prompt were opened;
  • your sound, music, fireplace and vibration settings (on or off);
  • an app-instance ID and a Firebase installation ID: random numbers created for this copy of the app, not linked to your name or any account, and replaced if you reinstall the app;
  • your device model, Android version and the app version;
  • an approximate location (country or region), worked out from your IP address; your precise location is never collected;
  • when the app crashes, details of the crash (what the app was doing, the device's state, such as free memory, and a Crashlytics installation ID).

The advertising ID is never read. We also use these usage statistics in Google Ads to measure how well our own advertising campaigns bring new players, for example how many players who installed the app went on to play a round. They are never used to personalise ads, and for players in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland they are not sent to Google Ads at all. Google handles this information under Google's Privacy Policy; Firebase explains its handling at Privacy and Security in Firebase.

What stays on your device

The app saves your game in progress, your new-game choices (such as the number of players and the computer level), your sound, music, fireplace and vibration settings, your record of wins, losses and draws against the computer and the badges it has earned, and a count of finished games and the day the first one finished, and the date the rating prompt was last shown (see Ratings prompt below) in the app's private storage on your device. This information stays on your device (only the on and off of the four sound and vibration settings is in the usage statistics above). If your phone backs up its apps to your Google account, Android includes all of it except the game in progress in that backup, which Google keeps for you under its own policies; we never see it. Uninstalling the app deletes it from the device.

Ads

The app is supported by ads from Google AdMob (a banner shown during games and a full-screen ad between rounds). Because children may use the app, every ad request is marked as child-directed and limited to a G (general audiences) content rating, and the app does not request or use the Android advertising ID, so ads are not personalised.

To deliver and measure ads, Google's advertising software may process technical information from your device, such as your IP address, device and app information, and diagnostics. This is handled by Google under its own policies; we do not receive this information. You can read about it in Google's Privacy Policy and how AdMob handles data.

The app uses the internet only to load ads, to send the usage statistics and crash reports (described above), to fetch a small settings file (described below) and, if you rate the app, Google Play; every game works without one.

Settings file

Each time it starts, the app downloads a small settings file from our page on GitHub Pages, a web hosting service run by GitHub. The file says which games the app shows, in what order, and how often ads may appear. The request contains nothing about you: no identifier, no account and no game data. Like any website, GitHub's servers see the IP address the request comes from and log it for security; GitHub handles this under its own privacy statement, and we do not receive it. If the download fails, the app uses the last settings it saved, or its built-in ones.

Ratings prompt

The app uses Google Play's In-App Review library, which may show Google's rating card after you have finished several games. If you rate, the rating goes to Google Play under Google's own policies; we do not receive anything from the card. The app only keeps on your device when it last asked and how many rounds have finished and the day the first one did, and sends nothing to us. The "Rate Boardly" tile in Settings simply opens the app's page in the Play Store.

Feedback by email

The Settings screen has a "Send feedback" button. It opens your own email app with a message to us, already filled in with the app version, the Android version and your phone's model, so we can see what a problem might depend on. Nothing is sent until you send the email yourself, and you can delete those lines first. The app itself sends nothing. When you do send it, we receive your email address and your message, and we use them only to answer you.

Children

The app is meant for a general audience aged 13 and over. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children: the usage statistics and crash reports above hold no name, email address or account. Ads are child-directed and use a general-audiences rating, as described above.

Sharing and selling

We do not sell or rent any information. The usage statistics and crash reports are processed by Google for us (Firebase), and the usage statistics are used in our own Google Ads account to measure our campaigns, as described above; we share them with no one else.

Security and your rights

The usage statistics and crash reports are not linked to your name or any account, so we cannot tell which of them are yours. Analytics keeps event data for 14 months, then deletes it. Uninstalling the app stops all collection; reinstalling it starts with new IDs. If you have a question or a request, contact us (below).

Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy (for example, by adding a feature that handles information), we will update this page and the date above before the change reaches you.
